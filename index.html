Timing Belt Calculator

Two-pulley drive · Nominal pitch geometry

Assumes an open, two-pulley drive without idlers. Verify actual pulley and flange clearance, available belt sizes, manufacturer engagement requirements and tensioning allowance. Calculated dimensions are nominal, not installation tolerances.

How the belt calculation works

This example uses an open GT2 belt with a 2 mm tooth pitch and two 20-tooth pulleys.

  1. Find the pulley pitch radii

    Multiply pulley teeth by belt pitch to get the pitch circumference, then divide by 2π to get the radius. In this example, each pitch circle is 20 × 2 = 40 mm around, with a radius of about 6.37 mm. The center distance is measured between the pulley axes.

  2. Add the straight spans and wrapped arcs

    With equal pulleys, each straight span is 100 mm long and the belt wraps halfway around each pitch circle. That gives 2 × 100 mm + 2 × 20 mm = 240 mm at the belt pitch line. With unequal pulleys, the spans slope and the larger pulley has more than half a turn of wrap.

  3. Convert pitch length to belt teeth

    Divide 240 mm by 2 mm per tooth to get 120 teeth. If the result is fractional, the calculator picks the nearest whole tooth count that fits without pitch-circle overlap. The adjustment shows how far to move the pulley centers from the requested distance to fit that belt.

  4. Work backward from a known belt

    Multiply 120 teeth by 2 mm to get the 240 mm target pitch length. The calculator uses bisection, repeatedly halving the range of possible center distances until the calculated belt path matches that length. The required center distance in this example is 100 mm.

Check stock belt sizes, actual pulley and flange clearance, and tensioning allowance before choosing a belt.

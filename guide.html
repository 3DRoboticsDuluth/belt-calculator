How timing belt calculations work

Find the belt tooth count or pulley center distance for an open, two-pulley drive. The worked example uses a GT2 belt, two 20-tooth pulleys, and 100 mm between their centers.

1. Understand belt pitch

Pitch (p) is the center-to-center distance between adjacent teeth along the belt. GT2 uses a 2 mm pitch and HTD 5M uses a 5 mm pitch. The GT2 preset shows a nominal 6 mm belt width. Width is measured across the belt and does not enter the length calculation.

2. Find the pulley pitch diameter

Multiply the pulley tooth count (N) by pitch to get the pitch circumference. Divide by π to get the pitch diameter (D). The pitch radius (r) is half that diameter.

D=N×pπ,r=D2

Each 20-tooth GT2 pulley has a 40 mm pitch circumference, a 12.73 mm pitch diameter, and a 6.37 mm pitch radius. The pitch circle is the effective circle used for belt travel. On a timing pulley, the pitch circle lies outside the physical tooth tips.

3. Break the belt path into four segments

The belt follows two straight spans tangent to the pitch circles and two curved arcs around the pulleys. With equal pulleys, each span is 100 mm long and each arc covers half the 40 mm pitch circumference.

4. Calculate total belt pitch length

Let C be the distance between the pulley axes, r1 and r2 the pitch radii, and d = |r2 − r1| the difference between them. The straight span and the radius difference form the legs of a right triangle with C as its hypotenuse. Each span is √(C² − d²) long.

Adding the two spans and both arcs gives the exact nominal open-belt equation used by the calculator. The inverse sine is in radians.

L=2C2−d2 +π(r1+r2) +2darcsin(dC)

For our equal pulleys, d = 0. The two spans total 200 mm, the two arcs total 40 mm, and the belt pitch length is 240 mm.

5. Convert pitch length to belt tooth count

Nbelt=Lp=240 mm2 mm per tooth=120

If the estimate is fractional, the calculator picks the nearest whole tooth count that clears the pitch circles. The nearby-sizes table solves for the required centers for each feasible belt. The adjustment is the required center distance minus the center distance you entered. A positive value moves the pulleys farther apart.

Work backward from a known belt

For a 120-tooth GT2 belt, the target length is 120 × 2 mm = 240 mm. With equal pulleys the equation simplifies to L = 2C + 40 mm, so C = (240 − 40) ÷ 2 = 100 mm.

The calculator uses bisection for all pulley sizes. It first checks that the target belt is longer than the path where the pitch circles just touch. It then starts with a center-distance range from the sum of the pitch radii to the target belt length.

Ltarget=Nbeltp,Cmid=Clow+Chigh2

At each midpoint, it evaluates the belt length. If the belt path is too long, it lowers the upper bound. Otherwise it raises the lower bound. Repeating this process converges on the required centers.

Pulley wrap and teeth in engagement

Set α = arcsin(d/C). The smaller pulley loses wrap and the larger pulley gains it. The angles below are in radians. Multiply by 180/π to convert to degrees.

θsmall=π−2α,θlarge=π+2α

Multiply the smaller pulley’s tooth count by its fraction of a full turn. In the example, 180° of wrap gives 20 × 180/360 = 10 teeth in engagement. The calculator flags estimates below six teeth. Check the belt manufacturer’s engagement requirement for your load.

Nengaged=Nsmallθsmall2π

Drive ratio

With pulley 1 driving pulley 2, divide the driven pulley tooth count by the driver tooth count. A 20-tooth driver and a 40-tooth driven pulley give 2:1 reduction, so the driven pulley turns at half the driver speed. The two 20-tooth pulleys in the example give 1:1.

Reduction=N2N1

Checks before choosing a belt

Stock and fit
Confirm that the belt is available in the required tooth profile, pitch, width, and tooth count, and that it matches both pulleys.
Clearance
The calculator requires C > r1 + r2. Check real flange, housing, and mounting clearances separately.
Tension and installation
Use the manufacturer’s tension specification and allow enough mount travel for installation and tensioning. The center distances are nominal geometry. Drives with idlers or crossed belts use different paths.
